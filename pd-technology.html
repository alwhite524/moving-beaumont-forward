Police technology dossier · Public briefing

Beaumont PD Technology

What technology Beaumont Police uses, what Council authorized, how the systems are governed, and what the public record still does not establish.

Updated August 22, 2026 · Evidence reviewed through August 2026

Executive summary

One accountable record for Beaumont PD technology

The verified record currently identifies four major technology areas: Flock fixed automated license plate readers, Axon body-worn cameras and digital-evidence services, a Flock Drone-as-First-Responder program, and the Peregrine investigative and analytics platform.

Each system has a different purpose and risk profile. Council authorization and vendor descriptions establish approved capabilities and contract ceilings—not current deployment, policy compliance, accuracy, effectiveness, or public-safety outcomes.

Evidence ruleAuthorization is not deployment. Contract value is not expenditure. Vendor capability is not verified performance.

Detailed technology records

Decisions, capabilities, costs and evidence

01 · ALPR

Flock automated license plate readers

A 2023 staff report traces Beaumont's arrangement to July 2020. Council approved citywide right-of-way authority in 2023 and a two-year subscription for 36 additional cameras in 2024.

$225,900 authorized over two years

Eleven existing cameras plus 36 additions implies 47, but the reviewed record does not confirm every unit was installed or remains active.

02 · BWC

Axon body-worn cameras

In December 2025, Council approved a five-year agreement combining body cameras with digital-evidence management, transcription, translation, redaction assistance, and equipment services.

Five-year agreement approved 5–0

The executed price schedule, total ceiling, invoices, deployed inventory, and operational audit record still need to be reconciled.

03 · DFR

Drone-as-First-Responder

In April 2026, Council approved a separate Flock aerial-response program. Approval does not establish its launch date, coverage, flight volume, privacy controls, or outcomes.

$900,000 authorized over three years

Aircraft inventory, FAA authority, flight logs, operating policy, incidents, staffing effects, invoices, and verified results remain outstanding.

04 · DATA

Peregrine investigative platform

In August 2026, Council approved software described as consolidating searches across approximately twelve law-enforcement data sources and supporting analytics and future drone workflows.

Approved 5–0 after public discussion

The connected data map, permissions, query logs, accuracy controls, total cost, invoices, and measurable outcomes are not yet established.

Cross-technology accountability

Questions every system must answer

  1. What is deployed today?Which hardware, software, accounts, integrations, launch sites, and vendor services are active?
  2. What data enters and leaves?What is collected, generated, retained, exported, shared, or deleted?
  3. Who can use it?Which users, supervisors, vendors, prosecutors, and outside agencies have access?
  4. Is it accurate and effective?What verified measures show benefits, errors, false alerts, complaints, misuse, or no operational value?
  5. What does it actually cost?What was authorized, contracted, invoiced, paid, and exposed to future renewal?
  6. How is the public protected?What policies, prohibited uses, audits, complaint procedures, security controls, and reporting apply?

Verified timeline

How the record developed

  1. July 2020

    Sundance arrangement

    A later staff report says Beaumont entered a Flock data-sharing arrangement for equipment in public right-of-way within the Sundance neighborhood.

  2. May 2023

    Citywide placement authority

    Council approved a ten-year agreement allowing Flock equipment in public rights-of-way throughout Beaumont.

  3. August 2024

    Thirty-six-camera expansion

    Staff reported 11 operating cameras. Council unanimously authorized 36 additions through a $225,900 two-year subscription.

  4. December 2025

    Axon ecosystem approved

    Council approved a five-year body-camera, digital-evidence, and software-services agreement.

  5. April 2026

    Drone program authorized

    Council unanimously approved a separate three-year, $900,000 Drone-as-First-Responder agreement.

  6. August 2026

    Peregrine platform approved

    After discussion, Council unanimously approved the investigative and analytics platform.
